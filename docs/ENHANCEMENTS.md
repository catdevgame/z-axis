# Enhancements: what the collection adds to each game

An enhancement is something the collection adds to a game: a settings page, a key, a camera, native saves.
Aggressive Inline's enhancements are the baseline: each one exists in DM2 and BMX XXX as well, done the way AI's is
(its source is in the table), at the game's own functions. Content that exists only because a release had it (AI's
Xbox textures, languages and French speech, the Zoo, Estate, the Ape) is not part of the baseline: a game gets the
like only where one of its own releases has it (DM2's Xbox levels, and DM2's and BMX XXX's own Xbox textures). An
enhancement that makes sense for one game alone goes in "Per game" below.

## When

An enhancement hooks one subsystem of the game (its camera, HUD, pause menu, saves, sound). Port it to a game once
that subsystem's functions are readable and Verified there. The enhancement then calls named, typed functions rather
than addresses, and the gate keeps proving the game unchanged with it off. The app's settings rows come from the
game's options in collection/settings.cpp (profiles()); a ported enhancement adds its option to the
game's profile.

Defaults change no game logic: each enhancement is off, or at the game's own value, until the player picks it.
logiccheck, dm2logic, bmxlogic and the A/B run with the defaults.

A view setting is drawn, not played. Widescreen and a field of view setting (AI's Field of View and Camera Xbox,
BMX XXX's Field of View) widen or narrow the drawn frustum and projection, while the game decides by its own view:
the PS2's 4:3 frustum at the game's field of view (zge/host/game_view.h: the scene walk marks a node drawn by it).

In Aggressive Inline what is on screen feeds play. The placed effects spawn (and draw random numbers) only while
their node is drawn, their area in view and the camera near enough; the NPCs and animators move only on screen; the
traffic stands still in regions out of view from the camera. So objects the drawn view adds at the sides behave as
the PS2's just off screen do. AI's Camera Distance and Height and mouse-look move the camera, and the camera's
position decides the regions and the effects' range. With either in use, a hidden follow camera at the game's own
tuning, the pad's stick and no mouse-look runs first each frame, outside the game's EE time (runtime/ee_time.h), and
its pose is the game's view (runtime/game_pose.h; aggressive_inline_slus20327.cpp), while the player's camera,
wall collision and all, is drawn. The Behind camera type's frame has its twin too (updateBehindCamera). The twin is
zge_view.h's CameraTwin.

BMX XXX's Camera Distance and Height move its action cameras (updateCameras). Their twin (bmx_xxx_view.cpp) keeps
the cameras, their objects and records and the riders with their models, and leaves the listeners, Doppler and blur
alone while it runs. What the player's cameras left is noted as their update returns, and a camera object's own
update marks only the bytes it writes, so every change the game makes to the cameras in between (the player's camera
made at the level's start, a cut) reaches the twin too. Its pose is the PS2's every frame, and the path men, which
animate and greet only while their node is drawn, do as on the PS2.
